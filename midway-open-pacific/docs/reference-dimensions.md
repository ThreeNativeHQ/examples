# Reference dimensions — Midway: Open Pacific

Reference table for PRD-midway-asset-battle-integration AC-2: every imported model is scaled to a
documented reference, and the measurement endpoints are recorded here. SI first, imperial in
parentheses. Knots are converted to metres per second at **1 kn = 0.514444 m/s** (all `m/s` figures
below are **derived** conversions). Anything labelled *derived* shows its arithmetic. Anything not
locatable in a cited source is written **not found** — never estimated.

Endpoints used: **length** is overall length (o/a) unless stated otherwise; **beam** is hull beam at
the waterline, not flight-deck or sponson width; **draught** is mean or full-load depth as the source
states. Where a source distinguishes waterline (wl), perpendiculars (pp) or flight-deck (fd)
dimensions, the field says which.

Primary sources are official history (NHHC / DANFS), the US Navy ordnance manuals (OP 747, OP 629(A)),
Japanese ordnance (OP 1507), museum aircraft pages, and — where no official figure exists —
long-established naval references (Navypedia, CombinedFleet, NavWeaps, the Pacific War Online
Encyclopedia). Hobby wikis are not used as primary; Wikipedia is cited only where it aggregates and
cites a primary source.

### 16. US Mark 14 submarine torpedo — 1942 service state

| Field | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 6.40 m (21 ft 0 in) | [Warfare History Network](https://warfarehistorynetwork.com/article/the-defective-mark-14-torpedo/) |
| Body diameter | 0.533 m (21 in) | [USNI](https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history-magazine/1992/december/technical-report-mk-xiv-torpedo-lessons-today) |
| Warhead | 230 kg (507 lb) TNT initially; 303 kg (668 lb) Torpex later | [USNI](https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history-magazine/1992/december/technical-report-mk-xiv-torpedo-lessons-today) |
| Speed settings | 31.5 kn = **16.21 m/s** (long range); 46 kn = **23.66 m/s** (short range) | [USNI](https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history-magazine/1992/december/technical-report-mk-xiv-torpedo-lessons-today) |
| Range at each speed | 8,230 m (9,000 yd) at 31.5 kn; 4,115 m (4,500 yd) at 46 kn | [USNI](https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history-magazine/1992/december/technical-report-mk-xiv-torpedo-lessons-today); [Naval Undersea Museum](https://navalunderseamuseum.org/mk-14/) |
| Running depth range | Set by pistol; nominally settable, but in 1942 the weapon **ran 3.0–3.4 m (10–11 ft) deeper than set** | [USNI](https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history-magazine/1992/december/technical-report-mk-xiv-torpedo-lessons-today); [Naval Submarine League](https://archive.navalsubleague.org/2002/the-mark-14-torpedo-tribulations) |

**1942 reliability problems (explicit).** Three compounding defects: (1) ran 10–11 ft deeper than the
set depth because the heavier warhead was never recalibrated into the depth mechanism; (2) the Mark 6
magnetic influence exploder detonated prematurely, up to ~15 m from a target; (3) the contact
exploder's firing pin sheared or failed to travel far enough on a square (90°) hit, producing duds —
glancing hits worked, dead-on hits did not. About 80 % of the ~800 torpedoes fired by mid-1942
failed. Fixes: re-calibrated depth (Aug 1942), magnetic feature ordered disconnected (June 1943), and
a lighter/stronger firing pin from scrapped aircraft propellers (late 1943).
([Warfare History Network](https://warfarehistorynetwork.com/article/the-defective-mark-14-torpedo/); [USNI](https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history-magazine/1992/december/technical-report-mk-xiv-torpedo-lessons-today))

### Endpoint integrity notes for AC-2

- **Yorktown:** do **not** use the 251.4 m flight-deck-overhang or the 251.58 m sister-ship mesh
  measurement; the adopted hull reference is 246.74 m.
- **I-168:** do **not** use the 98.40 m pp figure from Wikipedia's infobox; the adopted o/a reference
  is 104.7 m.
- **Tone / Mogami beams:** the adopted values are the waterline hull beams (18.5 m, 20.6 m); bulge and
  sponson widths are larger and are not the AC-2 endpoint.
- **Aircraft:** AC-2's principal span check uses wingspan; the TBD is the only subject whose design
  span (15.24 m) and hull length (10.67 m) are close enough to be confused at low model resolution.
